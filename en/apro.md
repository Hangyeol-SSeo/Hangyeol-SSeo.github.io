<h1 style="font-size: 2.8rem; margin-bottom:8px">Motivation Behind the Service</h1>

<p>
This started from the problem that amateur soccer enthusiasts have difficulty finding opponents of similar skill levels.
In 2023–2024, most amateur soccer teams found opponents through online communities (Naver, Daum Cafes) to schedule weekly matches.
Post templates included a section for team skill level, but ratings were highly subjective and often didn't reflect actual gameplay.
Additionally, stronger teams who struggled to find matches would understate their skill level, resulting in mismatched games.
<br/>
To address this, we planned to create a tier system for amateur soccer teams by region, matching teams of similar skill levels for more enjoyable games.
We also aimed to replace the legacy system of manually posting team info and contact details every week with something more convenient and efficient.
</p>

<h1 class="growth-section-header" style="font-size: 2.8rem; margin-bottom:8px">🌱 Hypothesis Validation Process</h1>

<div class="growth-card" style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 30px; border-radius: 12px; margin: 20px 0; font-size: medium">
  <h2 style="color: #222222">⚽️ Validating Whether the Problem Exists</h2>
  <b>Opportunity & Hypothesis</b>: "More than 20% of teams using matching communities find it difficult to match with teams of similar skill level" <br/>
  <b>Execution</b>: Built a web landing page and deployed it on matching communities as a fake-door pretotyping test — told users to enter team name and preferred date/time for automatic matching, then collected data <br/>
  <b>Results</b>: Out of ~500 unique visitors, 44.7% submitted their team information <br/>
  <b>Reflection</b>: Confirmed the problem is real; the response rate was higher than expected <br/>
</div>

<div class="growth-card" style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 30px; border-radius: 12px; margin: 20px 0; font-size: medium">
  <h2 style="color: #222222">👨🏻‍⚖️ Deriving a Solution → Surveying Demand</h2>
  <b>Opportunity & Hypothesis</b>: Introduce referees to fairly measure each team's skill → calculate team tiers based on measurements → match teams within the same tier <br/>
  <b>Execution</b>: Used Google Forms to survey willingness to referee introduction and whether referees could fairly assess skill levels <br/>
  <b>Results</b>: 23 teams responded; ~80% were in favor of introducing referees; generally positive reaction to fair skill assessment <br/>
  <b>Reflection</b>: Survey results were positive but real-world execution might differ; aimed to schedule test matches with hired referees ASAP <br/>
</div>

<div class="growth-card" style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 30px; border-radius: 12px; margin: 20px 0; font-size: medium">
  <h2 style="color: #222222">💸 Building a Business Model Around Referees</h2>
  <b>Opportunity & Hypothesis</b>: "More than 50% of teams would pay ₩45,000+ for matching fees + referee costs" (₩45,000 per team = ~₩3,000 per player for 15 players) <br/>
  <b>Execution</b>: Used Google Forms to survey willingness to pay for matching + referee services <br/>
  <b>Results</b>: Out of ~20 teams, 0 were willing to pay ₩45,000+. Maximum willingness was ₩30,000 <br/>
  <b>Reflection</b>: Per-team pricing felt expensive to respondents. Lowering the price would eliminate profitability, so alternative solutions needed to be explored <br/>
</div>

<div class="growth-card" style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 30px; border-radius: 12px; margin: 20px 0; font-size: medium">
  <h2 style="color: #222222">🏟️ Skill Assessment Experiments with Referees</h2>
  <b>Opportunity & Hypothesis</b>: Conducted 5 matches with hired referees and post-match satisfaction surveys <br/>
  <b>Execution</b>: Recruited 2 KFA-certified referees (Level 2 and Level 4); asked them to evaluate each team's skill after 2-hour matches <br/>
  <b>Results</b>: Satisfaction varied greatly depending on the referee; no team was willing to pay for the level of service. KFA-affiliated referees were prohibited from earning income through non-official matches. Some teams noted a positive effect on preventing overheated play <br/>
  <b>Reflection</b>: The referee income restriction made the solution impractical. Recruiting referees was extremely difficult. Concluded there was insufficient willingness to pay for this business model <br/>
</div>

<div class="growth-card" style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 30px; border-radius: 12px; margin: 20px 0; font-size: medium">
  <h2 style="color: #222222">🎤 On-Site Interviews</h2>
  <b>Opportunity & Hypothesis</b>: Verify whether the problem truly exists and understand what solutions people want, through in-person interviews <br/>
  <b>Execution</b>: Visited soccer fields around Anyang, Gyeonggi-do, 3–4 times per week, interviewing players. Recorded and organized interviews from ~60 teams <br/>
  <b>Results</b>: The severity of the problem varied significantly by team. Younger teams wanted solutions and were already exploring tournament participation. Teams aged 40+ said they just wanted to enjoy soccer and didn't see the problem as serious <br/>
  <b>Reflection</b>: In-person interviews were exhausting but became easier with practice. Learned the importance of clearly defining the problem and target market before testing multiple solutions <br/>
</div>
